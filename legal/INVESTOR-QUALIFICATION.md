## SEC Definition of Accredited Investor

### Legal Foundation
The accredited investor definition is codified in **Rule 501(a) of Regulation D** under the Securities Act of 1933. This classification determines eligibility for participation in certain exempt securities offerings, including private equity funds.

### Purpose
Only accredited investors may participate in certain securities offerings because they are presumed to be:
- Financially sophisticated
- Able to sustain the risk of loss
- Capable of protecting their own interests without the full disclosure protections of a registered offering

### Individual (Natural Person) Qualifications

An individual qualifies as an accredited investor by meeting **any one** of the following criteria:

#### Financial Criteria

**Income Test:**
- Earned income exceeding **$200,000** individually (or **$300,000** jointly with spouse or spousal equivalent) in **each of the prior two years**
- Reasonable expectation of the same income level for the current year
- Income sources include: wages, self-employment income, interest, dividends, capital gains, and other taxable income

**Net Worth Test:**
- Net worth exceeding **$1 million**, either individually or jointly with spouse or spousal equivalent
- **Excludes** the value of primary residence
- Must be calculated at the time of the securities purchase

**Net Worth Calculation Rules:**
- **Include:** Bank accounts, retirement accounts (401(k), IRA), investments, vehicles, other assets (excluding primary residence)
- **Exclude:** Fair market value of primary residence
- **Liabilities to include:**
  - All debts except mortgage up to the fair market value of primary residence
  - Any mortgage amount exceeding the fair market value of primary residence (underwater mortgage)
  - Any increase in home equity loan or line of credit in the **60 days prior** to purchase (prevents artificial inflation of net worth)

**Spousal Equivalent Definition:**
- A cohabitant occupying a relationship generally equivalent to that of a spouse
- Property need not be held jointly
- Securities being purchased do not need to be acquired jointly

#### Professional Certification Criteria

Individuals holding **in good standing** any of the following professional licenses:

1. **Series 7** – General Securities Representative License (FINRA)
2. **Series 65** – Licensed Investment Adviser Representative (NASAA)
3. **Series 82** – Private Securities Offerings Representative License (FINRA)

**Good Standing Requirements:**
- Current, active license
- No suspensions or revocations
- Compliance with all FINRA and applicable state rules

#### Relationship-Based Criteria

- **Directors, executive officers, or general partners** of the company selling the securities (or of a general partner of that company)
- **"Knowledgeable employees"** of the private fund being invested in
- Any **"family client"** of a qualifying "family office"

### Entity Qualifications

Entities may qualify as accredited investors through several pathways:

#### Investment-Based Qualification
- Entities owning **investments in excess of $5 million**
- Entity must not have been formed specifically to purchase the subject securities

#### Asset-Based Qualification
- The following entity types with **total assets exceeding $5 million**:
  - Corporations
  - Partnerships
  - Limited Liability Companies (LLCs)
  - Trusts
  - 501(c)(3) organizations
  - Employee benefit plans
  - "Family offices" and their "family clients"

#### Ownership-Based Qualification
- Any entity in which **all equity owners are accredited investors**
- Allows pass-through qualification

#### Financial Institution Qualification
Automatically qualified entities include:
- Banks
- Savings and loan associations
- Insurance companies
- Registered investment companies
- Business development companies
- Small business investment companies
- Rural business investment companies
- SEC-registered or state-registered investment advisers
- Exempt reporting advisers
- SEC-registered broker-dealers

#### Sophisticated Trust Qualification
- Any trust with **total assets exceeding $5 million**
- Not formed specifically to purchase the subject securities
- Purchase directed by a **"sophisticated person"**

**Sophisticated Person Definition:**
- Must have (or the issuer reasonably believes has) sufficient knowledge and experience in financial and business matters to evaluate the merits and risks of the prospective investment

## Verification Methods & Documentation

### Rule 506(b) vs. Rule 506(c) Standards

Private equity funds typically raise capital under one of two Regulation D exemptions, each with different verification standards:

| Aspect | Rule 506(b) | Rule 506(c) |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Solicitation** | No general solicitation or advertising | General solicitation and advertising permitted |
| **Verification Standard** | "Reasonable belief" | "Reasonable steps to verify" |
| **Strictness** | More flexible | More stringent |
| **Documentation** | Fact-specific analysis | Objective verification required |
| **Investor Limit** | Up to 35 non-accredited (if sophisticated) + unlimited accredited | **All investors must be accredited** |

### Rule 506(b): Reasonable Belief Standard

Under Rule 506(b), the fund must have a **"reasonable belief"** that each investor is accredited.

**Factors to Consider:**
- The fund's **relationship with the investor** (existing relationship vs. new investor)
- **Information available** about the investor
- **Context** of how the investor was introduced to the fund
- **Nature of the investor** (individual vs. entity; sophistication level)

**Acceptable Methods:**
- Review of investor's prior investment history with the fund
- Knowledge of investor's financial circumstances through business relationship
- Investor questionnaire with supporting representations
- Review of publicly available information (e.g., employment position, public filings)
- Information provided by trusted intermediaries (lawyer, accountant, broker-dealer)

**Important Note:**
- Self-certification alone (checking a box) is **NOT sufficient** without additional knowledge or information
- Must be based on reasonable inquiry given the facts and circumstances

### Rule 506(c): Reasonable Steps to Verify Standard

Under Rule 506(c), the fund must take **"reasonable steps to verify"** accredited status objectively.

#### Principles-Based Verification Method

The fund must make an **objective determination** that its verification steps are reasonable considering:

1. **Nature of the investor:**
   - Individual vs. entity
   - Type of accredited investor claimed (income, net worth, professional license, entity assets, etc.)

2. **Information available about the investor:**
   - Existing relationship or new investor
   - Prior knowledge of financial circumstances
   - Documentation already in possession

3. **Nature of the offering:**
   - How the investor was solicited (website, advertisement, social media, referral)
   - Terms of the offering (minimum investment amount, investment period)
   - Risk profile of the investment

**Documentation Standard:**
The verification method must be **objectively reasonable** to a third party examining the facts.

#### Non-Exclusive Safe Harbor Methods

Rule 506(c) provides specific safe harbor verification methods that, if followed, satisfy the verification requirement:

##### 1. Income Verification Method

**Documents Required:**
- IRS Form W-2 (wage and tax statement) for the **two most recent years**
- IRS Form 1099 (various income types) for the **two most recent years**
- IRS Schedule K-1 (partnership income) for the **two most recent years**
- Copy of individual's **filed federal income tax return** (Form 1040) for the **most recent year**

**Process:**
1. Obtain copies of income documentation for the two most recent years
2. Verify income exceeds $200,000 individually or $300,000 jointly
3. Obtain written representation from investor that they reasonably expect to reach the same income level in the current year

**Written Representation Should Include:**
- Confirmation of income levels for the prior two years
- Statement of reasonable expectation for current year income
- Acknowledgment that any material change in circumstances will be disclosed to the fund

##### 2. Net Worth Verification Method

**Documents Required (all dated within prior 3 months):**
- **Bank statements** (checking, savings, money market)
- **Brokerage statements** and account statements
- **Certificates of deposit**
- **Tax assessment** for real property holdings
- **Credit report** from at least one nationwide consumer reporting agency (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion)
- **Written representation** from the investor confirming:
  - All liabilities necessary to calculate net worth
  - That net worth exceeds $1 million (excluding primary residence)

**Process:**
1. Collect asset documentation dated within the prior three months
2. Review documentation to assess asset values
3. Obtain credit report to verify liabilities
4. Obtain written representation regarding liabilities and net worth calculation
5. Calculate net worth excluding primary residence and related mortgage (up to fair market value)
6. Verify net worth exceeds $1 million

**Primary Residence Exclusion Rules:**
- Value of primary residence: **excluded from assets**
- Mortgage on primary residence up to fair market value: **excluded from liabilities**
- Underwater mortgage (loan > fair market value): **excess amount counts as liability**
- Home equity line or loan increase within 60 days: **counts as liability** (prevents artificial inflation)

##### 3. Written Confirmation from Qualified Third Party

The fund may rely on **written confirmation** from any of the following professionals:

**Qualified Third Parties:**
- Registered **broker-dealer**
- SEC-registered **investment adviser**
- Licensed **attorney** (in good standing)
- Certified **public accountant (CPA)** (in good standing)

**Required Certification Content:**
The third party must provide written confirmation (dated within the **prior three months**) stating that they have:
1. **Taken reasonable steps to verify** the investor is an accredited investor
2. **Determined** that the investor qualifies as an accredited investor

**Sample Language:**
> "I, [Name], a [licensed attorney/CPA/registered investment adviser/registered broker-dealer], hereby certify that within the past three months I have taken reasonable steps to verify and have determined that [Investor Name] qualifies as an accredited investor under Rule 501 of Regulation D."

**Benefits:**
- Shifts verification burden to a qualified professional
- Particularly useful for high-net-worth individuals with complex financial situations
- Creates liability buffer for the fund

##### 4. Prior Verification Method (Five-Year Rule)

**Applicability:**
If the fund previously verified an investor as accredited using reasonable steps, it may rely on that prior verification for **five years**.

**Requirements:**
1. Original verification was conducted using reasonable steps (under Rule 506(c) standard)
2. Less than **five years** have elapsed since the original verification
3. Fund obtains a **written representation** from the investor at the time of the new sale confirming they still qualify
4. Fund has **no knowledge of information to the contrary** (no red flags indicating investor no longer qualifies)

**Process:**
1. Verify that prior verification was conducted and documented within the last five years
2. Obtain current written representation from investor
3. Confirm no knowledge of changed circumstances (job loss, bankruptcy, etc.)
4. Document reliance on prior verification in investor file

##### 5. Professional Certification Verification

For investors claiming accredited status through Series 7, 65, or 82 licenses:

**Verification Steps:**
1. Request copy of the investor's **professional license or certification**
2. Verify the license is **current and in good standing** through:
   - **FINRA BrokerCheck** (for Series 7 and 82): [https://brokercheck.finra.org](https://brokercheck.finra.org)
   - **SEC Investment Adviser Public Disclosure (IAPD)** (for Series 65): [https://adviserinfo.sec.gov](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov)
   - State securities regulator databases
3. Confirm no suspensions, revocations, or disciplinary actions
4. Document verification with screenshots or printouts dated at time of verification

**Important:**
"In good standing" means active, current license with no regulatory sanctions or disciplinary actions.

##### 6. Entity Verification Methods

**For Entity Investors Claiming $5 Million in Assets/Investments:**

**Documents Required:**
- Audited **financial statements** (most recent year)
- Unaudited **financial statements** (if audited unavailable, dated within prior three months)
- **Bank statements** and **brokerage statements** (dated within prior three months)
- **Tax returns** (most recent year)
- **Written representation** from authorized officer/partner confirming:
  - Entity assets or investments exceed $5 million
  - Entity was not formed for the specific purpose of investing in the fund

**Process:**
1. Request and review financial documentation
2. Verify entity has qualifying asset level
3. Confirm entity formation predates the offering (or has legitimate business purpose)
4. Obtain representation from authorized signatory
5. For foreign entities, may need translated documents and verification of authorization

**For Entity Claiming All-Equity-Owners-Are-Accredited:**

**Requirements:**
1. Obtain **list of all equity owners**
2. **Verify each equity owner** individually as an accredited investor using methods above
3. Obtain **written representation** that the list is complete and accurate
4. Document verification for each underlying beneficial owner

**Important Consideration:**
This method can be burdensome if there are many equity owners. Recommend requesting entity pursue alternative qualification method if feasible.

## Self-Certification vs. Third-Party Verification

### Self-Certification (Investor Representation)

**What It Is:**
- Investor completes a questionnaire or subscription agreement representing that they meet accredited investor criteria
- Typically includes checkbox attestations and signature
- May include supporting narrative explanations

**When It's Sufficient:**
- **Rule 506(b) offerings** where the fund has an existing relationship with the investor and other information supporting reasonable belief
- **Not sufficient alone** under Rule 506(c) without additional verification steps
- May be combined with other verification methods

**Limitations:**
- Self-certification **alone is never sufficient** for Rule 506(c) offerings
- Creates minimal legal protection for the fund
- Investor may not fully understand the definitions or calculation methods
- Risk of investor misrepresentation or mistake

**Best Practice:**
Always supplement self-certification with at least one additional verification method, even under Rule 506(b).

### Third-Party Verification

**What It Is:**
- Independent verification by a qualified professional (CPA, attorney, broker-dealer, or registered investment adviser)
- Professional conducts their own review and provides written certification
- Shifts verification responsibility to the third party

**Advantages:**
1. **Strong legal protection** for the fund
2. **Professional expertise** in evaluating complex financial situations
3. **Liability shield** – third party assumes responsibility for accuracy
4. **Regulatory comfort** – clear compliance with Rule 506(c)
5. **Investor privacy** – fund doesn't need to review detailed personal financial documents

**Disadvantages:**
1. **Cost** – investor or fund must pay for professional verification (typically $500-$2,500)
2. **Time** – may delay closing while third party completes verification
3. **Availability** – not all investors have existing relationships with qualified professionals
4. **Coordination** – requires communication between fund, investor, and third party

**When to Require Third-Party Verification:**
- Rule 506(c) offerings with general solicitation
- Investors introduced through advertising or unknown sources
- High-dollar investments ($1 million+)
- Investors with complex financial situations
- International investors
- When fund lacks other means of verification

### Comparative Risk Analysis

| Method | Legal Protection | Cost | Time | Investor Burden | Fund Burden | Recommended Use |
|--------|-----------------|------|------|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Self-Certification Only** | Low | None | Fast | Low | Low | Never recommended alone; supplement with other methods |
| **Documentation Review (Income)** | Medium-High | Low | Fast | Medium | Medium | Rule 506(c); straightforward income verification |
| **Documentation Review (Net Worth)** | Medium-High | Low-Medium | Medium | High | High | Rule 506(c); requires multiple documents and calculation |
| **Third-Party Verification** | High | High | Medium | Medium | Low | Rule 506(c); high-dollar investments; complex situations |
| **Prior Verification + Representation** | Medium | None | Fast | Low | Low | Re-investments within 5 years |
| **Professional License Verification** | High | None | Fast | Low | Low | Investors with Series 7/65/82 licenses |

### Hybrid Approach (Recommended)

**Best Practice for Most Private Equity Funds:**

1. **Initial Screening:** Self-certification questionnaire identifies claimed qualification method
2. **Risk-Based Verification:** Apply appropriate verification method based on:
   - Offering type (506(b) vs. 506(c))
   - Investment amount
   - Source of investor (existing relationship vs. general solicitation)
   - Complexity of investor's financial situation
3. **Tiered Requirements:**
   - **Tier 1** (Lower risk): Documentation review by fund
   - **Tier 2** (Moderate risk): Enhanced documentation + written representation
   - **Tier 3** (Higher risk): Require third-party verification

**Example Tiered Policy:**

- **Rule 506(b) + existing investor:** Self-certification + confirmation of no material change (Tier 1)
- **Rule 506(c) + straightforward W-2 income:** W-2 review + representation (Tier 1)
- **Rule 506(c) + net worth based:** Full documentation review (Tier 2)
- **Rule 506(c) + general solicitation + $1M+ investment:** Third-party verification required (Tier 3)
- **International investor:** Third-party verification preferred (Tier 3)

## Investor Qualification Questionnaire Language

### Purpose of the Questionnaire

The investor questionnaire serves multiple critical functions:
1. **Accredited investor verification** (primary purpose)
2. **Suitability assessment** (investor sophistication and risk tolerance)
3. **AML/KYC compliance** (identity verification and source of funds)
4. **Bad actor disqualification** (Rule 506(d) compliance)
5. **Regulatory compliance** (ERISA, tax status, foreign investor regulations)

## Subscription Agreement Overview

### Purpose of the Subscription Agreement

The subscription agreement is the **legal contract** between the investor and the fund. It serves to:

1. **Formalize the investment** (subscription for securities)
2. **Document investor representations and warranties**
3. **Establish legal terms and conditions**
4. **Ensure regulatory compliance**
5. **Create enforceable obligations**

Unlike the investor questionnaire (which gathers information), the subscription agreement is a **binding legal document** with contractual force.

### Key Components of a Subscription Agreement

A comprehensive subscription agreement for a private equity fund typically includes the following sections:

---

#### 1. Subscription Terms

**Essential Elements:**
- **Amount of investment** (subscription amount)
- **Form of investment** (capital commitment vs. funded amount)
- **Type of securities** (limited partnership interests, LLC membership interests, etc.)
- **Percentage interest** or number of units
- **Capital call provisions** (if applicable)
- **Closing date and conditions**

**Sample Language:**
> The undersigned (the "Investor") hereby irrevocably subscribes for and agrees to purchase [Units/Interests] in [Fund Name], a [State] [limited partnership/limited liability company] (the "Fund"), representing a capital commitment of [Commitment Amount] Dollars ($[Amount]) (the "Capital Commitment"), on the terms and subject to the conditions set forth in this Subscription Agreement and the Fund's [Limited Partnership Agreement/Operating Agreement] (the "Partnership Agreement").

---

#### 2. Payment Instructions

- **Payment method** (wire transfer, check, etc.)
- **Wire instructions** (bank details)
- **Payment deadline**
- **Capital call process** (for drawdown funds)
- **Currency** (if accepting non-USD)

---

#### 3. Representations and Warranties of the Investor

This is the most critical compliance section. The investor makes binding legal representations regarding:

**A. Accredited Investor Status**
> The Investor represents and warrants that it is an "accredited investor" as defined in Rule 501(a) of Regulation D under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended (the "Securities Act"), and the Investor has completed and delivered to the Fund an Investor Qualification Questionnaire evidencing such status.

**B. Investment Purpose**
> The Investor is acquiring the [Units/Interests] for the Investor's own account for investment purposes only and not with a view to, or for resale in connection with, any distribution or other disposition in violation of the Securities Act or any applicable state securities laws.

**C. Sophistication and Risk Understanding**
> The Investor (either alone or together with the Investor's purchaser representative) has such knowledge and experience in financial and business matters as to be capable of evaluating the merits and risks of the investment in the Fund, and the Investor is able to bear the economic risk of the investment, including the total loss thereof.

**D. Access to Information**
> The Investor has received and reviewed the Fund's [Private Placement Memorandum/Offering Documents] and has been afforded the opportunity to ask questions of, and receive answers from, the Fund's management regarding the Fund, its investment strategy, and the terms and conditions of the offering.

**E. No Solicitation**
> [For Rule 506(b) offerings only:] The Investor has a pre-existing relationship with the Fund or its affiliates and was not solicited through general solicitation or advertising.

**F. Financial Resources**
> The Investor has adequate means to provide for the Investor's current needs and personal or business contingencies, has no need for liquidity with respect to the investment in the Fund, and is able to bear the economic risk of the investment for an indefinite period of time.

**G. Unregistered Securities**
> The Investor understands that the [Units/Interests] have not been registered under the Securities Act or any state securities laws and are being offered and sold in reliance on exemptions from the registration requirements thereof, and such securities may not be offered, sold, pledged, or otherwise transferred except in accordance with the registration requirements of the Securities Act or pursuant to an applicable exemption therefrom.

**H. Illiquidity**
> The Investor understands that there is no public market for the [Units/Interests] and that it is unlikely that a public market will develop, that the [Units/Interests] are illiquid and subject to substantial transfer restrictions as set forth in the Partnership Agreement, and that the Investor may not be able to liquidate the investment even in the event of an emergency.

**I. No Guarantees**
> The Investor understands that no federal or state agency has passed upon the merits or risks of an investment in the Fund or made any finding or determination concerning the fairness or advisability of this investment.

**J. Reliance**
> The Investor acknowledges that the Fund is relying on the foregoing representations and warranties in accepting the Investor's subscription and admitting the Investor to the Fund.

## Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Considerations

### Regulatory Landscape for Private Equity Funds

Historically, private equity funds and their investment advisers were not subject to comprehensive AML/KYC obligations under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA). This changed significantly with FinCEN's adoption of a final rule on **August 28, 2024**, extending AML/KYC requirements to certain investment advisers managing private funds.

**Key Regulatory Development:**
- **Effective Date:** Originally January 1, 2026; **delayed until January 1, 2028**
- **Authority:** FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, U.S. Treasury)
- **Scope:** Investment advisers managing private funds are classified as "financial institutions" under the BSA
- **Oversight:** SEC will oversee examination authority for investment advisers

**Despite the delay to 2028:**
- Robust due diligence practices remain critical
- Many institutional investors already require AML best practices as a condition of investment
- International investors and cross-border funds may face earlier compliance requirements
- Implementing best practices now provides competitive advantage and reduces future compliance burden

### Core AML Requirements (Once Effective in 2028)

Private fund managers will be required to implement comprehensive AML/CFT (Counter-Financing of Terrorism) programs, including:

#### 1. Written AML/CFT Program

**Requirements:**
- Risk-based program tailored to the fund's specific risks
- Written policies and procedures
- Board/management approval and oversight
- Regular review and updates

**Key Components:**
- **Policies:** Detailed written procedures for all AML processes
- **Controls:** Internal controls to ensure ongoing compliance
- **Designation:** Appointment of an AML compliance officer
- **Training:** Ongoing AML training for relevant personnel
- **Testing:** Independent testing/audit of the AML program

**Risk Factors to Consider:**
- Types of investors (domestic vs. international; individuals vs. entities)
- Investment strategy and underlying portfolio (high-risk jurisdictions, industries)
- Distribution channels (direct vs. intermediaries)
- Transaction patterns (large cash investments, frequent redemptions)

---

#### 2. Customer Identification Program (CIP)

**Objective:** Verify the identity of each investor opening an account.

**Minimum Information to Collect:**

**For Individual Investors:**
- Full legal name
- Date of birth
- Residential address (physical address, not P.O. Box)
- Social Security Number (SSN) or Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)

**For Entity Investors:**
- Legal entity name
- Principal place of business (physical address)
- Employer Identification Number (EIN)
- Jurisdiction of formation/incorporation
- Formation documents

**Verification Methods:**

**Documentary Verification:**
- Government-issued ID (passport, driver's license, state ID)
- Articles of incorporation/organization
- Trust documents
- Partnership agreements

**Non-Documentary Verification** (when documentary unavailable or additional verification needed):
- Contacting the customer directly
- Checking references with other financial institutions
- Obtaining financial statements
- Credit bureau reports
- Public database searches

**Timing:**
- Verification must occur **within a reasonable time before or after** the investor's account is opened
- Generally, verification should be completed before acceptance of the subscription

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Record of identifying information obtained
- Description of verification method used
- Copy of identification documents reviewed (if applicable)
- Retention of records for **five years** after account closure

---

#### 3. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

**Objective:** Understand the nature and purpose of customer relationships to develop a risk profile.

**Core Requirements:**

**A. Identify and Verify Identity** (per CIP above)

**B. Identify and Verify Beneficial Owners**

For legal entity customers, identify and verify the identity of **beneficial owners**, defined as:

1. **Ownership Prong:** Each individual who owns, directly or indirectly, **25% or more** of the equity interests of the entity

2. **Control Prong:** A **single individual** with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct the entity (e.g., CEO, CFO, COO, Managing Member, General Partner, President)

**Information to Collect for Each Beneficial Owner:**
- Name
- Date of birth
- Residential address
- Social Security Number or other government ID number
- Copy of government-issued photo ID

**Exemptions:**
Beneficial ownership requirements do not apply to:
- Publicly traded companies (and their majority-owned subsidiaries)
- SEC-registered investment companies
- Financial institutions already subject to CIP requirements
- Governmental entities
- Banks
- Certain regulated entities

**C. Understand Nature and Purpose of Customer Relationship**

Conduct initial risk assessment to understand:
- Purpose of the investment
- Anticipated transaction activity
- Source of funds
- Expected behavior pattern

**D. Conduct Ongoing Monitoring**

- Monitor transactions and account activity for suspicious activity
- Update customer information periodically
- Implement risk-based procedures based on customer risk profile

**Risk-Based Approach:**
- **Low Risk:** Domestic individual investors with transparent income sources
- **Medium Risk:** Domestic entities, domestic investors with complex financial structures
- **High Risk:** Foreign investors, PEPs, investors from high-risk jurisdictions, complex ownership structures, unusually large investments

---

#### 4. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

For investors identified as **high-risk**, enhanced due diligence procedures must be implemented.

**High-Risk Categories:**
- **Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs):** Senior foreign political figures, their immediate family members, and close associates
- **High-Risk Jurisdictions:** Investors from countries identified by FATF as high-risk or non-cooperative
- **Complex Ownership Structures:** Multi-tiered entities with opaque ownership
- **Unusual Transaction Patterns:** Large cash investments, rapid turnover, inconsistent with stated purpose
- **Adverse Media:** Negative news, litigation, or reputational concerns

**EDD Procedures:**
- Obtain additional information on source of wealth and source of funds
- Obtain senior management approval for establishing or maintaining the relationship
- Conduct enhanced monitoring of account activity
- Perform periodic reviews (at least annually)
- Search adverse media and public records
- Understand business activities and economic rationale
- Document EDD findings and approval

**Source of Wealth vs. Source of Funds:**
- **Source of Wealth:** How the investor accumulated their net worth (e.g., inheritance, business sale, employment, investments)
- **Source of Funds:** Specific origin of the funds being invested (e.g., sale of stock, wire from bank account, liquidation of assets)

---

#### 5. Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)

**Obligation:** File SARs with FinCEN when known or suspected violations of federal law or suspicious transactions related to money laundering are detected.

**When to File a SAR:**
- Transactions involving at least **$5,000** (in the aggregate) where the fund:
  - Knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect the transaction involves funds derived from illegal activity
  - Knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect the transaction is designed to evade BSA requirements
  - Knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect the transaction has no apparent lawful purpose and the fund has no reasonable explanation after examination
  - Knows or suspects the transaction involves terrorist financing

**Red Flags for Suspicious Activity:**
- Investor reluctant to provide identifying information or provides false/suspicious information
- Investor makes unusually large investment inconsistent with known income/wealth
- Investor uses multiple entities or intermediaries to obscure source of funds
- Frequent changes to investment instructions or attempts to reverse transactions
- Investor requests unusual transaction structures designed to evade reporting
- Funds originate from high-risk jurisdictions or pass through multiple accounts
- Investment followed by rapid withdrawal requests
- Investor's stated business or occupation inconsistent with investment size
- Multiple investments in small amounts just below reporting thresholds (structuring)

**SAR Filing Requirements:**
- File **within 30 days** of initial detection of suspicious activity (or 60 days if no suspect is identified)
- File electronically through FinCEN's BSA E-Filing System
- **Do not notify** the subject of the SAR (strict confidentiality requirement)
- Maintain copy of SAR and supporting documentation for **five years**

---

#### 6. OFAC Sanctions Screening

**Authority:** Office of Foreign Assets Control (U.S. Treasury)

**Requirement:** Screen all investors (and beneficial owners) against OFAC sanctions lists to ensure compliance with economic and trade sanctions.

**Key OFAC Lists:**
- **Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) List:** Individuals and entities blocked under various sanctions programs
- **Sectoral Sanctions Identifications List (SSI):** Entities operating in sanctioned sectors
- **Foreign Sanctions Evaders List (FSE):** Individuals and entities evading U.S. sanctions
- **Non-SDN Lists:** Various program-specific lists

**Prohibited Transactions:**
- U.S. persons generally **cannot** engage in transactions with SDNs or blocked persons
- Cannot deal with persons in embargoed countries (e.g., Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, certain regions of Ukraine)
- Must block (freeze) any assets or property interests of blocked persons

**Screening Process:**
1. **Initial Screening:** Screen all investors at subscription against current OFAC lists
2. **Ongoing Screening:** Periodically re-screen existing investors (at least quarterly) as OFAC lists are updated
3. **Transaction Screening:** Screen parties to distributions and other transactions
4. **Automated Tools:** Use software tools for efficient screening (e.g., sanctions screening software)
5. **Documentation:** Maintain records of all screenings conducted

**Best Practices:**
- Screen using multiple identifiers (name, date of birth, address, passport number)
- Account for name variations, aliases, and transliterations
- Review potential matches carefully (false positives are common)
- Escalate matches to compliance officer or legal counsel
- If confirmed match: **reject the investment and file a report with OFAC**
- Subscribe to OFAC updates and implement immediate rescreening when lists are updated

**International Sanctions:**
For funds with international operations or non-U.S. investors, also consider:
- **UN Security Council sanctions**
- **EU sanctions** (if operating in EU)
- **UK sanctions** (if operating in UK)
- **Other jurisdictions** where the fund operates

---

#### 7. Recordkeeping

**Requirement:** Maintain detailed records of all AML compliance activities.

**Records to Maintain:**
- Customer identification information (CIP records)
- Beneficial ownership information
- Verification documents (copies of IDs, formation documents)
- Risk assessments and due diligence findings
- Enhanced due diligence documentation (for high-risk customers)
- OFAC screening results
- SARs filed and supporting documentation
- AML training records
- Independent audit/testing reports
- AML policies and procedures (and all prior versions)

**Retention Periods:**
- **Five years** after account closure (most records)
- **Five years** after SAR filing date
- **Five years** after transaction date (for transaction records)

**Accessibility:**
- Records must be readily accessible to examiners and regulators
- Implement organized filing system (physical or electronic)
- Consider cloud-based compliance management systems

---

#### 8. Training

**Requirement:** Provide ongoing AML training to all relevant personnel.

**Who Needs Training:**
- Compliance officers
- Investor relations personnel
- Operations staff involved in investor onboarding
- Senior management
- Anyone with investor contact or transaction processing responsibilities

**Training Content:**
- Overview of AML/CFT requirements and risks
- Fund's specific AML policies and procedures
- How to identify red flags and suspicious activity
- SAR reporting procedures
- OFAC sanctions compliance
- Beneficial ownership identification
- Documentation and recordkeeping requirements
- Consequences of non-compliance

**Frequency:**
- Initial training upon hire
- Annual refresher training for all personnel
- Additional training when policies/procedures change or new risks emerge

**Documentation:**
- Maintain records of all training provided
- Track attendance and completion
- Retain training materials and curricula

---

#### 9. Independent Testing/Audit

**Requirement:** Conduct regular independent testing or auditing of the AML program to ensure effectiveness.

**Frequency:**
- **At least annually** (more frequently if high-risk)
- Risk-based: higher-risk funds may need more frequent testing

**Scope of Testing:**
- Review of AML policies and procedures for adequacy
- Testing of CIP and CDD processes
- Review of high-risk account files
- Assessment of OFAC screening procedures
- Review of SAR decision-making and filing
- Evaluation of training program
- Review of recordkeeping practices
- Overall assessment of program effectiveness

**Who Conducts Testing:**
- Independent third party (external auditor, consultant)
- Internal audit department (if independent of compliance function)
- Cannot be conducted by the AML compliance officer or personnel responsible for the program

**Deliverables:**
- Written report of findings
- Identification of deficiencies
- Recommendations for improvement
- Management response and corrective action plan

---

### AML Best Practices for Private Equity Funds (Pre-2028 Implementation)

Even before the 2028 mandatory compliance date, funds should consider implementing AML best practices:

**1. Conduct Risk Assessment**

**6. Develop Policies and Procedures**
- Draft written AML policies tailored to the fund

**7. Market Advantage**

- Implementing best practices now can facilitate capital raising
- Demonstrates professionalism and regulatory sophistication

## Know Your Customer (KYC) Requirements

### KYC Overview

**Know Your Customer (KYC)** refers to the due diligence processes used to verify the identity of investors and assess their suitability. While closely related to AML compliance, KYC encompasses a broader set of practices.

### Distinction: AML vs. KYC

| Aspect | AML (Anti-Money Laundering) | KYC (Know Your Customer) |
|--------|----------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Focus** | Preventing illicit funds from entering the financial system | Understanding who the customer is and their legitimacy |
| **Regulatory Driver** | Bank Secrecy Act, FinCEN rules | Multiple regulations; best practice |
| **Scope** | Identity verification, suspicious activity monitoring, sanctions screening, SAR filing | Identity verification, background checks, source of wealth, ongoing relationship management |
| **Outcome** | Prevent money laundering and terrorist financing | Risk assessment and investor profiling |

**Relationship:**
KYC is often considered the foundation upon which AML compliance is built. Robust KYC processes enable effective AML compliance.

### Core KYC Requirements for Private Equity Funds

#### 1. Identity Verification

**Objective:** Confirm the investor is who they claim to be.

**Individual Investors:**
- Full legal name (as appears on government-issued ID)
- Date of birth
- Residential address (physical, not P.O. Box)
- Government-issued photo ID (passport, driver's license, state ID)
- Social Security Number or Tax ID

**Verification Methods:**
- Review original or certified copy of government-issued photo ID
- Compare photo to investor (if in-person meeting)
- For remote verification: video call with ID displayed, notarized copy, third-party verification service
- Cross-check address with utility bill, bank statement, or other documentation

**Entity Investors:**
- Legal entity name
- Jurisdiction of formation
- Formation date
- Entity type (corporation, LLC, partnership, trust)
- Tax ID (EIN)
- Principal place of business
- Formation documents (articles of incorporation/organization)
- Operating agreement, partnership agreement, or trust agreement
- Certificate of good standing (if applicable)
- Authorized signatories and their authority

**Verification Methods:**
- Review original or certified copies of formation documents
- Search Secretary of State database for entity status
- Obtain certificate of good standing
- Review board resolutions or member consents authorizing investment
- Verify authority of signatory through incumbency certificate or operating agreement

---

#### 2. Beneficial Ownership Identification

**Objective:** Identify the natural persons who ultimately own or control the entity investor.

**Requirements:**
For each legal entity investor, identify:
1. **Ownership:** Each individual who owns (directly or indirectly) **25% or more** of the entity
2. **Control:** At least one individual with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct the entity

**Documentation:**
- Beneficial ownership certification form
- Organizational chart showing ownership structure
- Government-issued photo ID for each beneficial owner (owning 25%+)
- Background information on control person

**Complex Structures:**
For multi-tiered entity structures:
- Trace ownership through each layer to identify ultimate beneficial owners
- Document ownership percentages at each tier
- If an entity in the chain is publicly traded or otherwise exempt, beneficial ownership search stops at that level

**Red Flags:**
- Refusal to provide beneficial ownership information
- Overly complex or opaque structures with no clear business rationale
- Use of nominee shareholders or bearer shares
- Frequent changes in ownership structure
- Ownership by entities in high-risk or secrecy jurisdictions (e.g., offshore tax havens without transparency)

---

#### 3. Source of Funds & Source of Wealth Verification

**Objective:** Understand where the investor's wealth and investment funds originated.

**Source of Wealth** (how they became wealthy):
- Employment income and position
- Business ownership and sale
- Inheritance or gift
- Investment returns
- Real estate holdings
- Professional practice (doctor, lawyer, etc.)

**Source of Funds** (origin of the specific investment amount):
- Sale of securities or assets
- Liquidation of bank/investment accounts
- Borrowing (loan proceeds)
- Business distributions
- Sale of property

**Documentation Requests** (risk-based):
- **Low Risk:** Self-attestation may be sufficient
- **Medium Risk:** Supporting documents (employment verification, business ownership proof, tax returns)
- **High Risk:** Detailed documentation (sale agreements, bank statements showing fund movement, audited financials, wealth manager confirmation)

**Risk-Based Triggers for Enhanced Verification:**
- Large investment amount relative to stated income/net worth
- Investor from high-risk jurisdiction
- Complex or unusual source of funds explanation
- Investor is a PEP or PEP-related
- Mismatch between stated occupation and investment size
- Funds originating from multiple sources or jurisdictions
- Unusual transaction structures

---

#### 4. Background Screening

**Objective:** Identify reputational, regulatory, or legal risks associated with the investor.

**Screening Components:**

**A. Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) Screening**
- Screen investors and beneficial owners against PEP databases
- Identify senior foreign political figures (current and former)
- Identify immediate family members of PEPs
- Identify close associates of PEPs

**B. Sanctions and Watchlist Screening**
- OFAC SDN list (U.S.)
- UN Security Council sanctions lists
- EU sanctions lists
- UK sanctions lists
- Interpol wanted persons
- Country-specific watchlists

**C. Adverse Media Screening**
- Search news databases for negative news
- Identify involvement in money laundering, fraud, corruption, financial crimes
- Identify civil or criminal litigation
- Identify regulatory enforcement actions
- Identify association with sanctioned persons or entities

**D. Regulatory and Disciplinary Actions**
- For investment professionals: FINRA BrokerCheck, SEC enforcement actions, state securities regulator records
- For business entities: SEC litigation releases, CFTC actions, Federal Trade Commission actions, Better Business Bureau complaints

**E. Credit and Bankruptcy**
- Credit reports (for individuals, if net worth verification method used)
- Bankruptcy searches (PACER, state courts)
- UCC lien searches (for entities)
- Judgment and lien searches

**Screening Tools:**
- Commercially available databases (LexisNexis, World-Check, Dow Jones Risk & Compliance, ComplyAdvantage)
- Manual searches (Google, news databases, court records)
- Professional background check services

**Documentation:**
- Date of screening
- Databases or sources searched
- Results (clear or hits identified)
- Analysis of any hits (false positive vs. true match)
- Decision rationale (accept, reject, or require additional due diligence)

---

#### 5. Risk Assessment and Categorization

**Objective:** Assign a risk rating to each investor based on collected KYC information.

**Risk Rating Categories:**
- **Low Risk:** Domestic, transparent investors with clear source of funds
- **Medium Risk:** Some complexity or foreign elements, but no significant red flags
- **High Risk:** PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, complex structures, adverse media, large investments

**Risk Factors to Consider:**

| Factor | Low Risk | Medium Risk | High Risk |
|--------|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Geography** | U.S. domestic | Developed countries (Canada, UK, EU, Australia) | High-risk jurisdictions per FATF, tax havens, sanctioned countries |
| **Investor Type** | Individual, transparent entity | Operating company, domestic trust | Complex multi-tier structure, offshore entity, bearer shares |
| **Source of Wealth** | Employment, publicly disclosed | Business ownership, investments | Unclear, inconsistent, PEP-related, high-risk industries |
| **Source of Funds** | Direct from known accounts | Intermediate transfers, multiple accounts | Circuitous routing, cash, high-risk jurisdictions, third-party sources |
| **PEP Status** | No PEP connection | Domestic PEP (lower risk) | Foreign PEP, family member, or close associate |
| **Adverse Media** | None | Minor negative news, civil disputes | Money laundering, fraud, corruption, criminal charges |
| **Investment Size** | Small to moderate | Moderate to large | Very large relative to fund size |
| **Transparency** | Fully transparent | Some opacity but explainable | Refusal to provide info, evasive answers |

**Risk-Based Procedures:**
- **Low Risk:** Standard CIP/CDD, periodic re-verification (every 3-5 years)
- **Medium Risk:** Enhanced documentation, more frequent monitoring (annually)
- **High Risk:** Full EDD, senior management approval, ongoing monitoring (quarterly or event-based), possible rejection

---

#### 6. Ongoing Monitoring

**Objective:** Ensure investor information remains current and identify changes that might elevate risk.

**Ongoing Monitoring Activities:**

**A. Periodic Information Updates**
- Request updated information from investors periodically (annually or every 2-3 years)
- Update investor questionnaires
- Refresh identity verification for high-risk investors
- Confirm no material changes to beneficial ownership, source of wealth, or risk profile

**B. Transaction Monitoring**
- Monitor capital calls and distributions for unusual patterns
- Identify changes in payment instructions (potential red flag)
- Monitor withdrawal requests and transfers (especially if frequent or large)
- Review investor communications for changes in circumstances

**C. Watchlist Rescreening**
- Periodically rescreen investors against updated OFAC and sanctions lists (at least quarterly)
- Implement real-time or near-real-time rescreening when lists update (best practice)
- Rescreen against adverse media periodically (annually for high-risk, less frequently for low-risk)

**D. Event-Driven Reviews**
- Trigger enhanced review if investor appears in adverse media
- Review if investor becomes a PEP
- Review if investor's country becomes subject to sanctions
- Review upon large additional investment or withdrawal request
- Review upon change in beneficial ownership

**E. Exit Monitoring**
- Conduct final AML/KYC review upon investor exit (redemption, transfer)
- Screen transferee/purchaser if investor sells interest
- Ensure compliance at exit with same rigor as at entry

---

#### 7. Record Retention

**KYC Records to Maintain:**
- All identity verification documents
- Beneficial ownership information
- Source of funds and source of wealth documentation
- Background screening results
- Risk assessments
- Ongoing monitoring records and updates
- Communications with investor regarding KYC matters
- Decisions to accept or reject investors

**Retention Period:**
- **Minimum 5 years** after relationship ends (consistent with BSA requirements)
- Consider longer retention for legal protection (6-10 years)
- Maintain records in organized, searchable format
- Ensure accessibility for audits and regulatory inquiries

### Best Practices Summary

#### 1. Offering Structure and Documentation

**✓ Choose the Appropriate Regulation D Rule**
- **Rule 506(b):** More flexible verification ("reasonable belief"), but no general solicitation. Best for funds raising capital from existing networks.
- **Rule 506(c):** Permits general solicitation but requires stricter verification ("reasonable steps to verify"). Best for funds using advertising or broad outreach.

**✓ Prepare Complete Offering Documents**
- Private Placement Memorandum (PPM)
- Limited Partnership Agreement or Operating Agreement
- Subscription Agreement
- Investor Qualification Questionnaire
- Risk acknowledgment and disclosure documents

**✓ Engage Experienced Securities Counsel**
- Have all offering documents drafted or reviewed by counsel experienced in private fund formation
- Ensure compliance with federal and applicable state securities laws
- Update documents regularly to reflect regulatory changes

**✓ Implement Blue Sky Compliance**
- Determine whether state securities law filings are required
- File Form D with SEC within 15 days of first sale
- File state notice filings or exemption claims as required
- Track which states have investors for ongoing compliance

---

#### 2. Investor Verification Process

**✓ Implement Tiered Verification Based on Risk**

| Investor Profile | Verification Approach |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Existing investor (re-investment within 5 years) | Prior verification + current representation |
| Domestic individual, W-2 income, personal relationship | Self-certification + income document review |
| Domestic individual, net worth based | Self-certification + net worth documentation review |
| Series 7/65/82 license holder | License verification via FINRA/IAPD + self-certification |
| Domestic entity, transparent ownership | Financial statements + self-certification |
| International investor, PEP, or complex structure | Third-party verification (CPA/attorney certification) |
| First-time investor from general solicitation (Rule 506(c)) | Enhanced verification (documentation or third-party) |

**✓ Document Verification Thoroughly**
- Create investor file for each subscriber
- Document verification method used and rationale
- Retain copies of all documents reviewed
- Note date of verification
- Record decision-maker who approved investor

**✓ Standardize the Process**
- Use checklists to ensure all steps completed
- Implement investor onboarding software/CRM for tracking
- Assign clear roles (who collects documents, who reviews, who approves)
- Set internal deadlines (e.g., verification complete within 5 business days of subscription receipt)

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

**1. Relying Solely on Self-Certification**
- Never accept checkbox attestation alone without any supporting information
- Especially problematic for Rule 506(c) offerings
- Creates significant legal risk if investor later proves not to be accredited

**2. Inconsistent Verification Methods**
- Applying different standards to different investors without documented rationale
- Invites claims of discrimination or favoritism
- Implement clear, objective, written policies

**3. Inadequate Documentation**
- Failing to document verification method used and why it was sufficient
- Not retaining copies of documents reviewed
- Creates problems during SEC examination or litigation

**4. Ignoring Beneficial Ownership**
- Accepting entity subscriptions without identifying ultimate beneficial owners
- Creates AML/KYC compliance gaps
- Increases risk of facilitating money laundering

**5. No OFAC Screening**
- Failing to screen investors against sanctions lists is a critical error
- Accepting a blocked person as investor creates severe legal liability
- Can result in asset freezing and penalties

**6. Weak Ongoing Monitoring**
- Treating compliance as one-time exercise at subscription
- Failing to update investor information or rescreen
- Missing changes in risk profile or sanctions status

**7. Insufficient Training**
- Staff not understanding verification requirements
- Inconsistent application of policies
- Mistakes in investor qualification determinations

**8. Poor Recordkeeping**
- Disorganized investor files
- Missing key documents
- Inability to produce records during audit

**9. Outdated Forms and Policies**
- Using old accredited investor definitions (pre-2020 amendments)
- Not updating for regulatory changes
- Relying on generic templates without customization

**10. Failing to Reject When Appropriate**
- Accepting investors who don't clearly qualify due to pressure to close
- Creates offering exemption failure risk
- Can invalidate entire offering
