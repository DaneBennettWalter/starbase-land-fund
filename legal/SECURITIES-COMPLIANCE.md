## SEC Regulation D Overview

### Purpose and Authority

Regulation D provides exemptions from the registration requirements of the Securities Act of 1933, enabling private companies to raise capital without going through the full SEC registration process. For real estate funds, Rules 506(b) and 506(c) are the most commonly used exemptions.

### Key Benefits

- **Unlimited Capital Raise:** Both Rule 506(b) and 506(c) permit raising an unlimited amount of capital
- **No SEC Registration:** Avoid the cost and time of full registration
- **Flexible Structure:** Choose between traditional private placement (506(b)) or public marketing (506(c))
- **Restricted Securities:** Securities sold are "restricted" and subject to holding periods before resale

### Legal Foundation

- **Rule 506(b):** Safe harbor under Section 4(a)(2) of the Securities Act
- **Rule 506(c):** Introduced by the JOBS Act of 2012
- **Governing Regulation:** 17 CFR § 230.506

## Rule 506(b) vs Rule 506(c) Comparison

### Rule 506(b) - Traditional Private Placement

#### Core Requirements

1. **NO General Solicitation or Advertising**
   - Strictly prohibited from any form of public marketing
   - Must have pre-existing, substantive relationships with investors
   - Cannot use unrestricted public websites to solicit investment

2. **Investor Eligibility**
   - **Unlimited** accredited investors
   - **Up to 35** non-accredited investors (must be "sophisticated")
   - Non-accredited investors must possess sufficient knowledge and experience in financial and business matters to evaluate the investment

3. **Accredited Investor Verification**
   - **"Reasonable Belief" Standard:** Issuer must have reasonable belief that investor is accredited
   - **Self-Certification Permitted:** Investors can self-certify their accredited status
   - Facts-and-circumstances analysis based on relationship and information available

4. **Disclosure Requirements**
   - **For Accredited Investors:** No specific disclosure mandated, but all information must be truthful and not misleading
   - **For Non-Accredited Investors:** Must provide disclosure documents similar to Regulation A or registered offerings, including:
     - Financial statements (may require certification or audit)
     - Any information provided to accredited investors must also be given to non-accredited investors

5. **Pre-Existing Relationships**
   - Must establish substantive relationship **before** discussing the investment opportunity
   - Relationship can be formed by the issuer, a broker-dealer, or investment adviser
   - "Substantive" means sufficient information to evaluate investor's accredited status

#### Advantages
- Less stringent verification requirements
- Can include up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors
- Lower compliance burden for established networks

#### Disadvantages
- Cannot broadly market the offering
- Limited to existing relationships or referrals
- Slower capital formation potential

---

### Rule 506(c) - General Solicitation Permitted

#### Core Requirements

1. **General Solicitation and Advertising PERMITTED**
   - Can publicly advertise through any channel:
     - Unrestricted public websites
     - Social media platforms
     - Television and radio
     - Print advertisements
     - Webinars and podcasts
     - Email campaigns
     - Public seminars

2. **Investor Eligibility**
   - **ONLY Accredited Investors** - No exceptions
   - Cannot sell to any non-accredited investors

3. **Accredited Investor Verification**
   - **"Reasonable Steps to Verify" Standard** - Cannot rely solely on self-certification
   - Must objectively verify accredited status through documentation
   - Significantly more stringent than Rule 506(b)

4. **Disclosure Requirements**
   - All information provided must comply with anti-fraud provisions
   - Must be truthful, complete, and not misleading
   - No specific disclosure format required for accredited investors

#### Advantages
- Broad public marketing permitted
- Faster capital formation potential
- Access to wider investor pool
- Modern digital marketing strategies allowed

#### Disadvantages
- Rigorous verification requirements
- Limited to accredited investors only
- Higher compliance costs for verification
- Cannot switch to 506(b) mid-offering

---

### **Important Note on Recent SEC Guidance (March 2025)**

The SEC issued significant guidance that **streamlines** the verification process under Rule 506(c) for high-dollar investments:

#### Simplified Verification for High-Dollar Investments

A fund can satisfy the "reasonable steps to verify" requirement through written representations alone if:

1. **Minimum Investment Thresholds:**
   - Individual investors: $200,000 minimum
   - Entity investors: $1,000,000 minimum

2. **Written Representations:**
   - Investor self-certifies accredited status
   - Investor represents that investment is not financed by a third party specifically for this investment

3. **No Actual Knowledge:**
   - Fund must not have actual knowledge that representations are false

**Impact:** This guidance significantly reduces the documentation burden for Rule 506(c) offerings with substantial minimum investment amounts, making it more attractive for institutional-grade real estate funds.

## General Solicitation Rules

### What Constitutes General Solicitation?

General solicitation includes any communication that:
- Conditions the market for a transaction
- Arouses public interest in a security
- Is made through impersonal, non-selective means

### Specific Examples of General Solicitation

**Prohibited under Rule 506(b):**
- Newspaper and magazine advertisements
- Unrestricted public websites featuring investment opportunities
- Television and radio broadcasts
- Public seminars (excluding qualifying demo days)
- Social media posts about the offering
- Email blasts to non-qualified lists
- Podcast advertisements
- Billboard or outdoor advertising

### How to Avoid General Solicitation (Rule 506(b) Only)

#### 1. Pre-Existing Substantive Relationship

**"Pre-Existing":**
- Relationship formed **before** the offering begins
- Established by issuer, broker-dealer, or investment adviser
- Cannot be created simultaneously with the investment discussion

**"Substantive":**
- Sufficient information to evaluate investor's financial sophistication
- Active evaluation of accredited investor status
- Ongoing relationship, not just a name in a database

#### 2. Qualifying Demo Day Events

Permitted without constituting general solicitation if:
- Sponsored by college, university, government, nonprofit, angel group, incubator, or accelerator
- Sponsor's role limited to hosting
- Advertising doesn't reference specific offerings
- Information limited to:
  - Notification of planned/ongoing offering
  - Type and amount of securities
  - Intended use of proceeds
  - Unsubscribed amount

#### 3. Password-Protected Website Portals

Common strategy for 506(b) offerings:
- Public-facing site shows only general company information
- Investment opportunities behind secure portal
- Access granted only after:
  - Completing qualification questionnaire
  - Establishing substantive relationship
  - Verification of accredited status

## Website Content Requirements

### For Rule 506(b) Offerings

#### ✅ PERMITTED on Public Website:

1. **General Business Information:**
   - Company name, mission, and history
   - Team biographies and qualifications
   - General description of business operations
   - Industry expertise and track record
   - Contact information

2. **Brand Building Content:**
   - Thought leadership articles
   - Market insights and research
   - Company news and achievements
   - Educational content about real estate investing
   - Press releases (not about the offering)

3. **Generic Investment Philosophy:**
   - General investment approach
   - Asset classes of interest (in general terms)
   - Geographic focus areas

#### ❌ PROHIBITED on Public Website:

1. **Offering-Specific Information:**
   - Fact that the fund is raising capital
   - Valuation or pricing
   - Minimum investment amounts
   - Targeted returns or projections
   - Deal structure or terms
   - Closing timelines
   - "Invest now" calls to action
   - Subscription procedures

2. **Investor Solicitation:**
   - Any language inviting investment
   - References to current fundraising
   - Investment opportunity descriptions
   - Links to subscription documents

**Golden Rule for 506(b):** If it could reasonably be viewed as inviting investment or creating an instant relationship with potential investors, it cannot be on the public website.

---

### For Rule 506(c) Offerings

#### ✅ PERMITTED on Public Website:

1. **All Content Allowed Under 506(b), Plus:**
   - Actual investment opportunities
   - Deal-specific information
   - Advertisement and solicitation language
   - Minimum investment amounts
   - Targeted returns and projections (subject to anti-fraud rules)
   - Fund terms and structure
   - Subscription procedures
   - "Invest now" calls to action

2. **Marketing Materials:**
   - Investment presentations
   - Performance data (must be accurate and not misleading)
   - Testimonials (with proper disclosures)
   - Video pitches and webinars

3. **Investor Portal Information:**
   - Access to detailed offering documents
   - Subscription agreement downloads
   - Investment calculator tools

#### ❌ STILL PROHIBITED:

1. **False or Misleading Information:**
   - Must comply with all anti-fraud provisions
   - Cannot make exaggerated claims
   - All projections must have reasonable basis
   - Past performance must include proper disclaimers

2. **Non-Accredited Investor Sales:**
   - Even with general solicitation, **cannot sell** to non-accredited investors
   - Website should clearly indicate accredited investor requirement
   - Must verify accreditation before accepting investment

3. **Unverified Claims:**
   - Cannot represent verification methods that aren't actually used
   - Must have documentation supporting all factual claims

**Key Advantage:** Rule 506(c) allows normal website content that would be expected for any investment opportunity, provided accredited investor verification is properly conducted.

## Accredited Investor Verification

### Definition of Accredited Investor

An "accredited investor" under Rule 501(a) includes:

#### Individual (Natural Person) Qualifications:

1. **Income Test:**
   - Annual income exceeding $200,000 (individual)
   - Annual income exceeding $300,000 (joint with spouse)
   - For the past two years with reasonable expectation of same in current year

2. **Net Worth Test:**
   - Net worth over $1,000,000 (individual or joint with spouse)
   - **Excluding primary residence value**
   - Calculated as assets minus liabilities

3. **Professional Credentials:**
   - Holders of Series 7, Series 65, or Series 82 licenses in good standing
   - Knowledgeable employees of private funds

#### Entity Qualifications:

- Banks, insurance companies, registered investment companies
- Business development companies
- Small business investment companies
- Certain employee benefit plans with assets exceeding $5 million
- Entities with total assets exceeding $5 million (not formed specifically to acquire the securities)
- Trusts with assets exceeding $5 million
- Entities where all equity owners are accredited investors

### Verification Methods: Rule 506(b)

#### "Reasonable Belief" Standard

**Key Principle:** Facts and circumstances analysis based on:
- Nature of relationship with investor
- Information available about investor
- Length and depth of relationship

**Acceptable Methods:**
1. **Self-Certification:**
   - Investor completes accredited investor questionnaire
   - Issuer has no reason to doubt the representations
   - Based on existing relationship and knowledge

2. **Existing Knowledge:**
   - Issuer knows investor's financial situation through prior relationship
   - Professional relationship indicates accredited status
   - Publicly available information (for public figures)

**Important:** Self-certification alone without any other knowledge of the investor's financial circumstances is **NOT sufficient**.

---

### Verification Methods: Rule 506(c)

#### "Reasonable Steps to Verify" Standard

**Key Principle:** Objective determination that steps taken are reasonable based on:
- Nature of the investor
- Type of accredited investor claimed
- Amount and type of information available
- Nature of the offering
- How investor was solicited
- Terms of the offering (e.g., minimum investment amount)

#### **Method 1: Principles-Based Approach**

Flexible approach considering all facts and circumstances. Examples:
- Long-standing relationship with extensive financial knowledge
- Professional status as investment adviser, broker-dealer, attorney
- Public information about investor's wealth or status

#### **Method 2: Income Verification**

Review copies of IRS forms showing income:
- Form W-2 (wage statements)
- Form 1099 (various income types)
- Schedule K-1 (partnership income)
- Form 1040 (tax returns)
- **Must be from past two years**
- Obtain written representation of reasonable expectation for current year

#### **Method 3: Net Worth Verification**

Review documentation dated within **prior three months:**
- Bank statements
- Brokerage statements
- Certificates of deposit
- Tax assessments for real property
- Credit report from nationwide consumer reporting agency
- Appraisals by licensed third parties
- **Plus:** Written representation from investor regarding net worth

**Must exclude primary residence:**
- Calculate as: Total Assets - Total Liabilities - Primary Residence Equity
- If primary residence has negative equity, must subtract that amount

#### **Method 4: Third-Party Written Confirmation**

Obtain written confirmation from qualified third party stating:
- They have taken reasonable steps to verify accredited status within last three months
- They have determined the investor is accredited

**Qualified third parties:**
- Registered broker-dealer
- SEC-registered investment adviser
- Licensed attorney (in good standing)
- Certified Public Accountant (in good standing)

**Requirements for third party:**
- Must be independent (not controlled by issuer)
- Must actually perform verification (not just repeat investor representation)
- Confirmation must be current (within three months)

#### **Method 5: Previously Verified Investors**

For investors previously verified as accredited:
- Obtain written representation at time of current sale
- Representation that investor still qualifies as accredited
- Valid for **five years** from date of previous verification
- **Requires:** Issuer has no information to the contrary

#### **Method 6: Streamlined Verification (March 2025 Guidance)**

For high-dollar investments:
- Individual minimum: $200,000
- Entity minimum: $1,000,000
- Written self-certified representations
- Representation that investment not financed specifically for this purpose
- No actual knowledge of false representations

**Best Practice:** This method significantly reduces burden and should be considered for institutional-quality real estate funds.

---

### Verification Documentation and Retention

**Critical Compliance Requirement:**
- Maintain complete records of verification methods used
- Document reasoning for determining "reasonable steps"
- Retain copies of all verification documents
- Create audit trail for each investor

**Recommended Documentation:**
- Verification checklist for each investor
- Copies of all documents reviewed
- Written determination of accredited status
- Date of verification
- Method(s) used
- Name of person conducting verification

## Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) Requirements

### Purpose of the PPM

The Private Placement Memorandum is the primary disclosure document that:
- Informs potential investors about the investment opportunity
- Discloses all material risks and terms
- Complies with anti-fraud provisions of securities law
- Provides legal protection to the issuer
- Sets forth the terms and conditions of the investment

**Important:** While not legally required under Regulation D, the anti-fraud provisions of federal securities law necessitate providing investors with truthful and accurate information. A comprehensive PPM is the standard method of compliance.

### Legal Framework

**Anti-Fraud Requirements:**
- Rule 10b-5 (Securities Exchange Act of 1934)
- Section 17(a) (Securities Act of 1933)
- State securities laws (Blue Sky)

**Disclosure Standards:**
- All information must be truthful and complete
- No misleading statements or omissions
- Material facts must be disclosed
- Risks must be specifically tailored to the offering

---

### Core Components of a Real Estate Fund PPM

An institutional-grade PPM typically exceeds 100 pages and includes:

#### 1. **Cover Page and Important Notices**

**Contents:**
- Fund name and legal entity structure
- Total offering amount and minimum investment
- Date of PPM
- Regulatory exemption being relied upon (e.g., Rule 506(c))
- Notice that securities have not been registered with SEC
- Notice that securities are "restricted" and cannot be readily resold
- Risk warning statements
- Notice that PPM is confidential and proprietary

**Standard Disclaimers:**
- "These securities have not been registered under the Securities Act of 1933"
- "These securities are subject to restrictions on transferability and resale"
- "Prospective investors should not construe the contents of this document as legal, tax, or financial advice"

#### 2. **Executive Summary**

**Contents:**
- Brief overview of investment opportunity
- Key terms at a glance
- Investment highlights
- Summary of fund structure
- Management team overview
- Target returns and timeline

**Purpose:** Provide quick reference for sophisticated investors while directing them to detailed sections for complete information.

#### 3. **Offering Summary and Terms**

**Required Information:**
- Legal name of fund entity
- Type of entity (LP, LLC, etc.)
- Total capital raise target
- Minimum and maximum offering amounts
- Minimum investment per investor
- Target closing date(s)
- Subscription period
- Form of investment (membership interests, limited partnership interests, etc.)
- Regulatory exemption (Rule 506(b) or 506(c))
- State notices and qualifications

#### 4. **Fund Overview and Investment Strategy**

**For Real Estate Funds:**
- **Asset Class Focus:**
  - Property types (multifamily, industrial, retail, office, mixed-use, etc.)
  - Development stage (stabilized, value-add, opportunistic, ground-up development)
  - Position in capital stack (equity, preferred equity, mezzanine, debt)

- **Geographic Scope:**
  - Target markets and submarkets
  - Rationale for geographic selection
  - Market analysis and trends

- **Investment Criteria:**
  - Property characteristics
  - Acquisition price ranges
  - Deal sourcing methods
  - Underwriting standards
  - Due diligence process

- **Asset Management:**
  - Property management approach
  - Value-add strategies
  - Lease-up and stabilization plans
  - Capital improvement programs

- **Hold Period and Exit Strategy:**
  - Projected hold periods
  - Exit strategies (sale, refinance, portfolio sale, etc.)
  - Market timing considerations
  - Liquidity events

- **Financing Strategy:**
  - Leverage targets and limits
  - Lender relationships
  - Debt terms and structures
  - Interest rate risk management

#### 5. **Risk Factors**

**Critical Requirement:** This is often the longest and most important section. Risks must be **specific to the offering**, not generic boilerplate.

**Real Estate-Specific Risks:**

**Market Risks:**
- Local and national real estate market volatility
- Economic recession or downturn
- Changes in supply and demand dynamics
- Competitive market conditions
- Property value fluctuations

**Property-Specific Risks:**
- Physical condition and deferred maintenance
- Environmental contamination
- Seismic, flood, or natural disaster risks
- Title defects or encumbrances
- Zoning and land use restrictions
- Entitlement risks for development projects

**Financial Risks:**
- Leverage and financing risks
- Interest rate fluctuations
- Inability to refinance or obtain financing
- Insufficient cash flow to cover debt service
- Capital call risks
- No guarantee of returns

**Operational Risks:**
- Property management challenges
- Tenant default or bankruptcy
- Lease-up risks (vacancy)
- Operating expense increases
- Inability to raise rents as projected
- Construction cost overruns
- Construction delays
- Contractor default

**Liquidity Risks:**
- No public market for securities
- Restrictions on transfer
- Limited or no redemption rights
- Long-term illiquid investment
- Difficulty in selling property assets

**Legal and Regulatory Risks:**
- Changes in tax laws
- Changes in securities laws
- Compliance costs
- Litigation risks
- Fair housing and discrimination claims

**Conflicts of Interest:**
- GP compensation and fees
- Related-party transactions
- Allocation of opportunities among funds
- Personal investments by management

**Key Person Risk:**
- Dependence on specific individuals
- Death, disability, or departure of key persons
- Succession planning limitations

**Sponsor/Manager Risks:**
- Limited operating history
- Past performance not indicative of future results
- Attribution of past performance

**Fund Structure Risks:**
- Tax consequences
- ERISA considerations
- Limited voting rights
- Control by General Partner

**Current Event Risks (Update as Relevant):**
- Economic uncertainty
- Supply chain disruptions
- Inflation impacts
- Labor shortages
- Government policy changes
- Public health emergencies

**Best Practice:** Include 20-40+ specific risk factors tailored to the actual fund strategy, properties, and market conditions. Generic risks should be avoided.

#### 6. **Management Team and Track Record**

**General Partner / Sponsor Information:**
- Detailed biographies of all principals
- Educational background
- Professional experience and history
- Real estate specific experience
- Track record of prior investments
- Years of experience in target markets/property types

**Attribution Methodology:**
- Clear explanation of how past performance is attributed to team members
- Specific roles in prior transactions
- Investment amounts and returns
- Whether principals were lead sponsors or participants
- Time period of involvement

**Decision-Making Structure:**
- Investment committee composition
- Voting procedures
- Approval thresholds
- Escalation procedures for decisions

**Key Person Provisions:**
- Definition of "key persons"
- Events triggering key person provisions
- Consequences (e.g., suspension of investment period)
- Replacement procedures

**Disclaimer Required:**
"Past performance is not indicative of future results. There can be no assurance that the Fund will achieve comparable results or that investors will not lose money."

#### 7. **Fund Terms and Economics**

**Management Fee:**
- Percentage rate (typically 1-2% annually)
- Calculation basis (committed capital, invested capital, NAV)
- Payment frequency
- Waiver or reduction provisions
- Offset provisions

**Carried Interest / Promote:**
- Percentage to GP (typically 20% for opportunistic RE funds, 10-20% for value-add)
- Calculation methodology
- Timing of payment (deal-by-deal or fund-level)
- Clawback provisions
- Crystallization events

**Preferred Return / Hurdle Rate:**
- Annual percentage (typically 6-10% for real estate)
- Whether compounded
- Calculation method
- Catch-up provisions
- Lookback provisions

**Waterfall Distribution Model:**
Complete description of distribution priority:

**Example Structure:**
1. First, return of capital to Limited Partners
2. Second, preferred return to Limited Partners (e.g., 8% annually, compounded)
3. Third, "catch-up" to General Partner until GP receives 20% of total distributions
4. Thereafter, 80% to Limited Partners, 20% to General Partner

**Diagrams helpful:** Include visual waterfall diagrams.

**Capital Contributions:**
- Initial capital commitment
- Capital call procedures
- Notice periods
- Failure to fund consequences (default provisions)
- Reserve requirements

**GP Co-Investment:**
- Required GP commitment amount or percentage
- Terms applicable to GP investment
- Alignment of interests statement

**Other Expenses:**
- Organization and offering expenses
- Operating expenses charged to fund
- Property-level expenses
- Deal fees (acquisition, disposition, financing)
- Third-party service provider fees

#### 8. **Use of Proceeds**

**Detailed Breakdown:**
- Property acquisitions (percentage)
- Reserves for capital improvements
- Reserves for operating expenses
- Reserves for debt service
- Organization and offering expenses
- Management company setup costs
- Legal and accounting fees
- Placement agent fees (if applicable)

**Investment Timeline:**
- Investment period duration
- Expected pace of deployment
- Deployment strategy

**Example Table:**
```
Property Acquisitions:                 85%
Capital Improvement Reserves:          8%
Operating Reserves:                    3%
Organization/Offering Expenses:        4%
TOTAL:                                100%
```

#### 9. **Subscription Mechanics and Investor Eligibility**

**Minimum Investment:**
- Stated minimum per investor
- Ability to accept lesser amounts at GP discretion
- Considerations for meeting new SEC streamlined verification thresholds

**Closing Schedule:**
- Initial closing date
- Subsequent closing dates
- Final closing date
- Right to extend or terminate offering

**Capital Call Procedures:**
- Notice period (typically 10-30 days)
- Payment instructions
- Acceptable forms of payment
- Consequences of non-payment

**Investor Eligibility Requirements:**
- Accredited investor requirement (for 506(c))
- Accredited or sophisticated requirement (for 506(b))
- Suitability standards
- Entity eligibility
- ERISA/benefit plan restrictions
- Non-U.S. investor restrictions
- Bad actor disqualification checks

**Subscription Process:**
- Subscription agreement execution
- Investor questionnaire completion
- Accredited investor verification documentation
- Payment of capital commitment
- Acceptance by General Partner (discretionary)

#### 10. **Legal and Regulatory Disclosures**

**Federal Securities Law Compliance:**
- **Exemption Relied Upon:**
  - "This offering is being made in reliance on Rule 506(c) of Regulation D under the Securities Act of 1933"
  - Explanation of exemption requirements
  - Consequences of non-compliance

- **Form D Filing:**
  - Notice that Form D will be filed with SEC
  - Timing of filing (within 15 days of first sale)
  - Availability through EDGAR

- **No SEC Registration:**
  - Securities have not been registered
  - No SEC review or approval
  - SEC does not pass upon merits of offering

- **Restricted Securities:**
  - Securities are "restricted" under Rule 144
  - Cannot be resold unless registered or exempt
  - Holding period requirements
  - No public market exists
  - Legends will be placed on certificates

**State Securities Law Compliance (Blue Sky):**
- States where offering is registered/noticed
- States where exemptions are claimed
- State-specific disclosure requirements
- Instruction to verify with state securities regulator
- Notice filing status

**ERISA Considerations:**
- Definition of "benefit plan investor"
- Percentage limitations to avoid plan asset treatment
- Prohibited transaction concerns
- Fiduciary requirements
- Recommendation to consult ERISA counsel

**Tax Considerations:**
- General tax treatment (partnership, LLC)
- Pass-through taxation
- K-1 distribution timing
- State and local tax obligations
- Foreign investor withholding (FIRPTA)
- Recommendation to consult tax advisors
- No tax advice provided disclaimer

**Anti-Money Laundering (AML):**
- Compliance with USA PATRIOT Act
- Investor verification requirements
- Beneficial ownership identification
- Right to reject suspicious investments

#### 11. **Financial Information and Reporting**

**Initial Financial Statements:**
- Fund has no operating history (for new fund)
- Pro forma financial projections (if included, with significant disclaimers)
- Financial statements of sponsor/GP (if applicable)

**For Non-Accredited Investors (Rule 506(b) only):**
- Financial statements similar to Regulation A requirements
- May require certification or audit by independent accountant
- Must be made available for review

**Post-Investment Reporting:**
- Quarterly unaudited financial statements
- Annual audited financial statements
- Property-level performance reports
- Capital account statements
- Distribution statements
- Annual tax information (Schedule K-1)

**Investor Access Rights:**
- Right to inspect books and records
- Limitations on access
- Procedures for requesting information

#### 12. **Subscription Procedures**

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Review PPM and all exhibits
2. Complete Subscription Agreement
3. Complete Accredited Investor Verification Documents
4. Complete Investor Questionnaire (including tax information)
5. Execute all signature pages
6. Submit to Fund Administrator or GP
7. Await acceptance notification
8. Fund capital commitment upon acceptance

**Required Documents (Attachments to PPM):**
- Subscription Agreement
- Investor Questionnaire
- Accredited Investor Verification Forms
- Operating Agreement or Limited Partnership Agreement
- Power of Attorney (if required)

### Disclosure Requirements by Investor Type

#### For Accredited Investors Only (Both 506(b) and 506(c))

**No Specific Format Required, But:**
- All information provided must comply with anti-fraud provisions
- Must be free from false or misleading statements
- Cannot omit material information
- Should provide sufficient information for informed decision

**Best Practice:** Provide comprehensive PPM to all investors regardless of accredited status to ensure uniform disclosure and stronger legal defense.

#### For Non-Accredited Investors (Rule 506(b) Only)

**Mandatory Disclosure:**
- Documents generally equivalent to Regulation A or registered offering
- **Financial Statements Required:**
  - Balance sheet as of 120 days before offering
  - Profit and loss statement for two years or life of company
  - Changes in financial position for same period

**Financial Statement Standards:**
- If offering up to $2 million: Financials need not be certified by accountant if unavailable without unreasonable effort/expense
- If offering $2-7.5 million: Financials certified by accountant required
- If offering over $7.5 million: Audited financials required

**Information Parity:**
- Any information provided to accredited investors must be provided to non-accredited investors
- Cannot create information asymmetry between investor classes

---

### PPM Updates and Amendments

**Requirement to Update:**
- Material changes to fund terms
- Changes to management team
- Additional risk factors identified
- Changes in regulatory compliance
- Significant adverse events

**Sticker Supplements:**
- Brief addendums addressing specific changes
- Attached to PPM or delivered separately
- Must be provided to all prospective investors
- Should be provided to existing investors if material

**Best Practice:** Date control is critical. Use version numbers and dates on every page. Track distribution to know which investors received which version.

## State Blue Sky Law Considerations

### Overview of Blue Sky Laws

**Definition:** State-level securities regulations that run parallel to federal securities law, requiring additional compliance at the state level.

**Origin:** Named after regulations designed to prevent "speculative schemes which have no more basis than so many feet of blue sky."

**Key Principle:** Even when exempt from federal registration under Regulation D, issuers typically must comply with state securities laws in every state where:
- The issuer is located
- Investors are located
- Offers are made

### Federal Preemption Under NSMIA

**National Securities Markets Improvement Act of 1996 (NSMIA):**
- Provides federal preemption of state registration requirements for "covered securities"
- Rule 506(b) and 506(c) offerings are "covered securities"
- **Result:** States cannot require registration of the securities offering itself

**However, States CAN Still Require:**
1. **Notice Filings:** Filing of Form D and payment of fees
2. **Consent to Service of Process:** Appointing agent for legal process in the state
3. **Anti-Fraud Enforcement:** Full authority to investigate and prosecute fraud

### State-by-State Requirements

**Critical Compliance Point:** Requirements vary significantly by state. There is no uniform standard.

#### Common State Requirements:

**1. Notice Filing (Form D)**
- File copy of federal Form D with state securities regulator
- Timing: Typically within 15 days of first sale to state resident
- Some states require filing before any offers in the state

**2. Filing Fees**
- Range from $0 to $1,500+ per state
- Some states charge percentage of amount raised from state residents
- Fee schedules vary widely

**3. Consent to Service of Process**
- Form appointing agent in state to receive legal documents
- Required in most states
- Often included with Form D filing

**4. State-Specific Disclosure Requirements**
- Some states require additional disclosures beyond federal requirements
- Certain states have specific legends or warnings required
- Cover page modifications may be needed

**5. Financial Statement Requirements**
- Some states impose additional financial disclosure
- May require audited financials even for accredited-only offerings

#### States with Notable Requirements:

**California:**
- Requires filing before first offer or sale
- $300 filing fee (as of 2026)
- May require additional disclosure for certain offerings

**New York:**
- Notice filing required
- Investment adviser registration may be triggered
- Relatively strict enforcement

**Texas:**
- Notice filing within 15 days of first sale
- $300 filing fee
- Consent to service of process required

**Florida:**
- Notice filing required
- Filing fee based on amount raised
- Must update on annual basis

**Each State Has Its Own Portal and Procedures:**
- No centralized federal filing for state notices
- Must file individually with each state
- Use state-specific forms or accept Form D

### Compliance Strategy

#### Recommended Approach:

**1. Pre-Offering Analysis:**
- Identify all states where offers will be made
- Determine states where investors are likely to be located
- Research specific requirements for each target state

**2. Filing Coordination:**
- Prepare master Form D for federal filing
- Prepare state-specific versions if needed
- Collect all required state forms
- Budget for filing fees across all states

**3. Timing:**
- Federal Form D: Within 15 days of first sale of securities
- State Form D: Check each state (may be before first offer)
- Maintain filing log to track deadlines

**4. Track Investor Locations:**
- Collect state of residence during subscription
- File notices as investments are accepted
- Update if previously unfiled states become relevant

**5. Ongoing Compliance:**
- Some states require annual renewal
- Update filings if offering is extended
- File amendments for material changes

**6. Engage Legal Counsel:**
- Multi-state offerings are complex
- Securities counsel familiar with blue sky laws essential
- Consider engaging local counsel in key states

### State Anti-Fraud Enforcement

**Important:** While NSMIA preempts state registration, states retain full authority to:
- Investigate potential securities fraud
- Bring enforcement actions
- Impose civil and criminal penalties
- Issue cease and desist orders

**State Regulators Actively Investigate:**
- Misleading marketing materials
- Failure to disclose material information
- Misrepresentation of risks or returns
- Unlicensed broker-dealer activity
- Unregistered investment adviser activity

**Best Practice:** Assume full state review of all offering materials and comply with highest standards across all jurisdictions.

## Form D Filing Requirements

### Purpose of Form D

Form D is a notice filing with the SEC that:
- Provides basic information about the offering
- Identifies the issuer and its management
- Notifies regulators of the private placement
- Creates public record searchable on EDGAR

**Important:** Form D is **not** a registration statement. It does not contain detailed financial or business information and does not constitute SEC approval or review.

### When to File Form D

**Timing Requirements:**
- Must be filed electronically with the SEC
- **Within 15 days after the first sale of securities** in the offering
- "Sale" occurs when investor irrevocably commits to purchase

**Definition of "First Sale":**
- Date when investor is legally bound to purchase securities
- Typically when subscription agreement is executed and accepted
- Payment does not need to be received for sale to occur

### Form D Contents

Form D is relatively brief (6 pages) and includes:

#### **Part A - Issuer Information**
- Issuer's name, address, telephone
- Legal entity type (LP, LLC, Corporation, etc.)
- Year of incorporation/formation
- Jurisdiction of incorporation
- Website address

#### **Part B - Principal Place of Business**
- Physical address of main operations
- Cannot be P.O. Box

#### **Part C - Related Persons**
- Officers, directors, and promoters
- Name, address, relationship to issuer
- Professional credentials

#### **Part D - Offering Information**
- Total offering amount (or "indefinite")
- Amount sold thus far
- Minimum investment amount (if applicable)
- Type of filing (new, amendment, or annual amendment)

#### **Part E - Type of Securities Offered**
- Equity, debt, pooled investment fund interests, etc.
- Business combination transaction checkbox
- Minimum investment accepted from any investor

#### **Part F - Exemption(s) Claimed**
- Check box for Rule 506(b) or Rule 506(c)
- Other exemptions if applicable (rare for real estate funds)

#### **Part G - Type of Filing**
- New notice
- Amendment to previous filing
- Annual amendment

#### **Part H - Duration of Offering**
- Date of first sale
- Expected completion date (if known)
- More than one year checkbox

#### **Part I - Industry Group**
- Select primary industry (e.g., "Pooled Investment Fund")
- Real estate funds typically select "Pooled Investment Fund - Real Estate"

#### **Part J - Issuer Size (Revenue Range)**
- Select applicable revenue range
- Not applicable for funds with no operations

#### **Part K - Federal Exemption and Exclusion**
- Investment Company Act exclusion claimed (typically 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7))

#### **Part L - Sales Compensation**
- Whether sales commissions or finder's fees paid
- If yes, list entities and individuals receiving compensation

#### **Part M - Offering and Sales Amounts**
- Total offering amount
- Total amount sold
- Total remaining to be sold

#### **Part N - Number of Investors**
- Number of investors who have already invested

#### **Part O - Sales to Existing Security Holders**
- Whether offering being made to existing security holders

#### **Part P - State Notice Information**
- List each state where notice is being filed
- Fee amounts for each state

### Filing Process

**Electronic Filing Required:**
1. Create account on SEC's EDGAR system
2. Obtain EDGAR access codes (CIK, CCC, Password)
3. Complete Form D using EDGAR filing platform or approved third-party software
4. Submit electronically
5. Receive confirmation of filing

**EDGAR Resources:**
- SEC Filer Manual: https://www.sec.gov/edgar/filer-information
- EDGAR Filing Website: https://www.edgarfiling.sec.gov/
- Support: (202) 551-8900

### Amendments to Form D

**Required Amendments:**
- Material changes to offering information
- Changes to issuer information
- Correction of errors or inadvertent omissions

**Annual Amendment Requirement:**
- If offering extends beyond one year from first sale
- Due within 30 days of anniversary of first sale
- Update all information to reflect current status

**Optional Amendments:**
- Update offering amounts as additional sales occur
- Add additional related persons
- Correct non-material information

**Best Practice:** File amendments to keep public record current, even when not strictly required. Demonstrates good faith compliance.

### Public Access to Form D

**EDGAR Database:**
- All Form D filings are publicly available
- Searchable by company name, CIK, or industry
- Provides transparency to regulators and potential investors

**Accessible At:**
- https://www.sec.gov/edgar/searchedgar/companysearch.html

**Information Available to Public:**
- Issuer name and address
- Related person names
- Offering amount and amount sold
- Type of exemption claimed
- States where offering is made

**Privacy Note:** Individual investor information is **not** included in Form D filing.

## Anti-Fraud Provisions

### Federal Anti-Fraud Rules Apply Regardless of Exemption

**Critical Principle:** Even when exempt from registration under Regulation D, issuers are **always** subject to anti-fraud provisions of federal securities laws.

### Primary Anti-Fraud Statutes

#### **1. Section 17(a) of the Securities Act of 1933**

Prohibits any person from:
- Employing any device, scheme, or artifice to defraud
- Obtaining money or property by means of any untrue statement of material fact or omission of material fact
- Engaging in any transaction, practice, or course of business which operates as a fraud or deceit upon the purchaser

**Applies to:** Offers and sales of securities

#### **2. Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5**

Rule 10b-5 makes it unlawful to:
- Employ any device, scheme, or artifice to defraud
- Make any untrue statement of a material fact or omit to state a material fact necessary to make statements not misleading
- Engage in any act, practice, or course of business which operates as a fraud or deceit

**Applies to:** Any purchase or sale of securities

#### **3. Section 12(a)(2) of the Securities Act**

Creates liability for:
- Offering or selling securities by means of a prospectus or oral communication
- That includes an untrue statement of material fact or omits a material fact necessary to make statements not misleading

**Private Right of Action:** Investors can sue to recover losses

---

### What Constitutes Fraud

#### **Material Misrepresentation:**

A statement is "material" if there is a substantial likelihood that a reasonable investor would consider it important in making an investment decision.

**Examples:**
- Falsely representing past performance
- Overstating returns or understating risks
- Misrepresenting qualifications or experience
- Fabricating property valuations or appraisals
- False statements about fund terms or fee structure
- Misrepresenting regulatory compliance status

#### **Material Omission:**

Failure to disclose information that is necessary to make statements not misleading.

**Examples:**
- Failing to disclose conflicts of interest
- Omitting material risks
- Not disclosing legal or regulatory problems
- Hiding poor performance of prior investments
- Failing to disclose related-party transactions
- Not disclosing key person departures

#### **Half-Truths:**

Statements that are technically true but misleading because they omit important context.

**Example:**
"The sponsor has acquired over $500 million in real estate."
- Misleading if fails to mention that most acquisitions were as a junior participant, not lead sponsor
- Misleading if properties were later foreclosed or lost

---

### Standard of Care: What Issuers Must Do

#### **Affirmative Duties:**

1. **Tell the Truth:**
   - All factual statements must be accurate
   - Verify facts before making representations
   - Maintain documentation supporting claims

2. **Disclose Material Information:**
   - Investors must receive information necessary for informed decision
   - When in doubt, disclose
   - Risk factors must be specific and tailored

3. **Avoid Misleading Statements:**
   - Context matters - technically true statements can still be misleading
   - Consider what reasonable investor would understand
   - Address information asymmetry

4. **Update Information:**
   - Disclose material changes that occur during offering period
   - Supplement PPM if necessary
   - Notify existing investors of material developments

#### **Due Diligence Requirements:**

Issuers (and their counsel) must conduct reasonable investigation:
- Verify representations about properties
- Confirm financial information
- Check backgrounds of management team
- Review appraisals and third-party reports
- Validate market data and assumptions

---

### Projections and Forward-Looking Statements

**General Rule:** Projections and forecasts are permitted but must:
- Have a reasonable basis in fact
- Disclose assumptions underlying projections
- Include prominent disclaimers
- Not be presented as guarantees

**Safe Harbor for Forward-Looking Statements:**
- Private Securities Litigation Reform Act of 1995 provides safe harbor
- Requires "meaningful cautionary statements"
- Must identify factors that could cause actual results to differ
- Limited applicability to private offerings

**Best Practice for Real Estate Funds:**
- Provide range of scenarios (base, optimistic, pessimistic)
- Clearly state that projections are not guarantees
- Disclose sensitivity to key assumptions
- Include disclaimer: "There is no assurance that projected returns will be achieved"

**Red Flags to Avoid:**
- Guaranteeing returns
- Using past performance to predict future results without disclaimers
- Cherry-picking favorable scenarios
- Basing projections on unrealistic assumptions

---

### Marketing Materials and Websites

**All Marketing Subject to Anti-Fraud Rules:**
- Websites
- Pitch decks
- Email communications
- Social media posts
- Advertisements (for 506(c))
- Video presentations
- Investor calls and webinars

**Consistency Required:**
- Marketing materials must be consistent with PPM
- Cannot contradict or oversimplify PPM disclosures
- If marketing highlights opportunities, must also reference risks
- Same level of care required as in PPM

**For Rule 506(c) with General Solicitation:**
- Advertising must not be misleading
- Performance data must be accurate and contextualized
- Cannot make exaggerated or unsubstantiated claims
- Must be able to substantiate all factual claims

---

### Testimonials and Endorsements

**SEC Marketing Rule Considerations:**

If fund manager is a registered investment adviser:
- Subject to SEC's Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1)
- Strict requirements for testimonials and endorsements
- Must disclose compensation
- Cannot cherry-pick favorable testimonials
- May require performance disclosures

**For Non-Registered Advisers:**
- Still subject to general anti-fraud rules
- Testimonials must be genuine and not fabricated
- Cannot be misleading or taken out of context
- Should disclose if compensation paid

---

### Track Record and Performance Advertising

**Attribution Issues:**

When advertising past performance:
- Must accurately attribute results to the individuals involved
- Cannot claim credit for transactions where role was minimal
- Must disclose if performance is from employment at another firm
- Should explain capacity (lead sponsor, co-sponsor, employee, consultant)

**Gross vs. Net Returns:**
- Must be clear whether returns are before or after fees
- Best practice: Show both gross and net
- If showing gross only, must disclose that fees will reduce returns

**Time Periods:**
- Cannot cherry-pick favorable periods
- Should show consistent time periods
- Disclose if time period is not representative

**Required Disclaimers:**
"Past performance is not indicative of future results. There is no guarantee that any investment will achieve its objectives or avoid losses."

---

### Insider Trading and Tipping

**Rule 10b-5 Prohibits:**
- Trading on material, non-public information
- Tipping others to enable them to trade
- Applies to all securities, including private fund interests

**For Real Estate Funds:**
- Management must maintain confidentiality about upcoming acquisitions, dispositions, or material events
- Cannot trade fund interests or related securities based on inside information
- Cannot selectively disclose material information to certain investors

### Enforcement and Penalties

#### **Civil Enforcement by SEC:**
- Cease and desist orders
- Injunctions
- Disgorgement of ill-gotten gains
- Civil monetary penalties
- Officer and director bars

#### **Criminal Prosecution:**
- Department of Justice can prosecute securities fraud
- Criminal penalties include fines and imprisonment
- No requirement to prove intent for certain violations

#### **Private Rights of Action:**
- Investors can sue for damages under Section 10(b), Rule 10b-5, and Section 12(a)(2)
- Can recover losses caused by fraud
- May recover attorneys' fees and costs

#### **State Enforcement:**
- State securities regulators can bring enforcement actions
- State criminal fraud statutes may apply
- Parallel state and federal actions possible

### Compliance Best Practices

**To Avoid Anti-Fraud Violations:**

1. **Maintain Documentation:**

   - Supplement PPM when material changes occur
   - Update website and marketing materials to stay current
   - Notify investors of significant developments

5. **Training:**

   - Create culture of compliance

6. **Supervise Marketing:**
   - Review all communications before distribution
   - Monitor sales team communications
   - Control who can speak on behalf of fund

## Compliance Recommendations

### Decision Matrix: Rule 506(b) vs. Rule 506(c)

#### **Choose Rule 506(b) If:**

✅ You have strong existing investor network  
✅ You want to include select non-accredited but sophisticated investors  
✅ You prefer privacy (no public marketing)  
✅ You can establish pre-existing relationships efficiently  
✅ Verification documentation burden is concern  
✅ Target investors are high-net-worth individuals with existing relationships

**Website Strategy for 506(b):**
- Public site: Company information, team, general philosophy only
- Private portal: Investment opportunities behind login
- Establish relationship before granting portal access
- Use qualification questionnaire and screening

---

#### **Choose Rule 506(c) If:**

✅ You want ability to market publicly  
✅ You can commit to rigorous verification process  
✅ You're comfortable with accredited-only limitation  
✅ You want to leverage digital marketing and social media  
✅ You're willing to pay for third-party verification services  
✅ You can meet streamlined verification thresholds ($200K+ individual, $1M+ entity)

**Website Strategy for 506(c):**
- Can show full investment opportunities publicly
- Include deal details, returns, terms
- Clear disclosure that only accredited investors can invest
- Integrated verification process
- Robust accreditation verification system before accepting funds

**Recommendation for Institutional-Quality Real Estate Funds:**

Given the **March 2025 SEC streamlined verification guidance**, **Rule 506(c) is increasingly attractive** for funds with:
- Minimum investments of $200,000+ (individual) or $1,000,000+ (entity)
- Professional management team
- Desire for broader marketing reach
- Capability to implement verification systems

The simplified verification process removes much of the historical disadvantage of 506(c) while preserving the marketing flexibility.

### Red Flags to Avoid

**These Actions Create Significant Legal Risk:**

❌ **Making guarantees of returns**
- No investment can guarantee returns
- Projections must include disclaimers

❌ **Cherry-picking performance data**
- Must present complete, fair picture
- Cannot highlight only successful investments

❌ **Failing to disclose conflicts of interest**
- Related-party transactions must be disclosed
- Compensation arrangements must be transparent

❌ **Using investor funds for unintended purposes**
- Use of proceeds must match PPM
- Cannot change without amendment

❌ **Accepting non-accredited investors in 506(c) offering**
- Strict prohibition, no exceptions
- Verification failure is not defense

❌ **General solicitation in 506(b) offering**
- Immediately disqualifies exemption
- All sales may become void

❌ **Failing to file Form D**
- Creates regulatory issues
- May impact ability to use exemption in future

❌ **Ignoring state securities laws**
- States actively enforce
- Can result in cease and desist orders

❌ **Making false or misleading statements**
- Criminal and civil liability
- Potential prison time for fraud

## Conclusion

Operating a private equity real estate fund website requires careful navigation of federal and state securities laws. The choice between Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) fundamentally shapes what can be displayed publicly and how investors can be solicited.

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Rule 506(b):** Private offering, no public solicitation, must establish relationships first, less stringent verification
2. **Rule 506(c):** Public marketing permitted, accredited only, rigorous verification required
3. **Recent SEC Guidance:** Streamlined verification for high-dollar investments ($200K+ individual, $1M+ entity) makes 506(c) more attractive
4. **Website Content:** 506(b) limited to general information; 506(c) can show investment opportunities
5. **Anti-Fraud Rules:** Apply regardless of exemption - all information must be truthful, complete, and not misleading
6. **PPM:** Comprehensive disclosure document essential for legal protection and investor information
7. **Blue Sky Laws:** State compliance required in addition to federal exemption
8. **Form D:** Must be filed within 15 days of first sale with SEC and applicable states
9. **Ongoing Compliance:** Regular updates, amendments, and filings required throughout offering period
10. **Professional Guidance:** Securities counsel essential for compliance and risk mitigation

**Critical Final Recommendation:**

**Do not launch a real estate fund offering or website without retaining experienced securities counsel.** The legal and financial consequences of non-compliance are severe, including:
- Civil penalties and disgorgement
- Criminal prosecution
- Rescission rights for investors (return of all capital)
- Personal liability for managers
- Permanent bar from securities industry
- Reputational damage

The cost of proper legal guidance is a small fraction of the risk of getting it wrong.

## Appendix A: Glossary of Terms

**Accredited Investor:** An investor meeting specific financial thresholds under Rule 501(a), generally including individuals with income over $200K or net worth over $1M (excluding primary residence).

**Blue Sky Laws:** State-level securities regulations requiring notice filings, fees, and compliance in each state where offerings are made.

**Form D:** Notice filing with the SEC providing basic information about a Regulation D offering.

**General Solicitation:** Public advertising or marketing of securities offerings through media, websites, or impersonal communications.

**NSMIA:** National Securities Markets Improvement Act of 1996, which preempted state registration requirements for certain covered securities.

**Pre-Existing Substantive Relationship:** Relationship formed before an offering begins, with sufficient information to evaluate investor qualifications.

**Private Placement Memorandum (PPM):** Comprehensive disclosure document providing detailed information about an investment offering.

**Reasonable Belief (506(b)):** Standard requiring issuer to reasonably believe investor is accredited based on facts and circumstances.

**Reasonable Steps to Verify (506(c)):** Objective standard requiring issuer to take active steps to verify accredited investor status through documentation.

**Regulation D:** SEC regulation providing exemptions from registration for private placements.

**Restricted Securities:** Securities that cannot be freely resold without registration or exemption due to holding period requirements.

**Sophisticated Investor:** Non-accredited investor with sufficient knowledge and experience to evaluate investment merits and risks.
